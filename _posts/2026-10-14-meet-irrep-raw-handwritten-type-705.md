When a music poster or rebellious branding concept demands the gritty friction of a felt-tip pen, digital letterforms often feel too clean and sterile. Solving this friction is the core mission of irrep, a handwritten typeface driven by erratic vertical pressure and jagged baseline shifts. Its low contrast strokes and playful grungy mood recreate the authentic imperfection of rapid marker writing directly on paper, giving designers a tool that refuses to look manufactured.

Built in a single regular weight with OTF, TTF, WOFF, and WOFF2 formats included, this font drops neatly into modern design software and web projects alike. It ships with language support for English and Zulu, allowing studios to push distinct messaging across diverse markets. The character set relies on raw energy rather than traditional typographic polish.

Deploy this asset when headlines need teeth, from loud product packaging and labels to stark flyers and logotypes. It handles tight tracking well, letting the erratic baselines lock together into a dense, frantic texture that demands attention without relying on gimmicks.
