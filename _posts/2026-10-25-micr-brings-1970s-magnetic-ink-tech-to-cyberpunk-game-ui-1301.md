Building fictional operating systems, cybernetic telemetry screens, or esports broadcast overlays often stalls when modern tech typefaces look too clinical or familiar. Micr solves this by reviving the mechanical oddity of vintage bank check encoding, translating magnetic ink typography into a dedicated display tool for modern screens. Rooted in 1970s and 1980s computing experiments, this all-caps family injects raw, blocky hardware personality directly into game titles, interface widgets, and identity systems.

The design relies on low-contrast, heavily geometric letterforms that echo automated machine-reading hardware. Across its five styles—spanning regular and medium weights in both normal and extended proportions—the glyphs maintain rigid horizontal baselines and distinct optical cutaways. These wide, structural forms keep compact HUD callouts sharp and readable under heavy digital chromatic aberration, scanline filters, or screen-printed poster graphics.

Available in OTF, TTF, WOFF, and WOFF2 formats, the family integrates into print apparel runs as well as web-based interface prototyping. Character sets cover Latin layouts for localized design, including German, Tagalog, Spanish, and Indonesian. Whether mapped across an arcade cabinet banner or locked into a streaming tournament scoreboard, Micr provides the tactile visual weight of early computing without compromising contemporary production pipelines.
