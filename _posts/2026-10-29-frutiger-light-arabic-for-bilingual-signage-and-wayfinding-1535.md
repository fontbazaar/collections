Designing international transit hubs and civic environments requires bilingual signage that balances Arabic and Latin scripts without visual disruption. Frutiger Light Arabic resolves this friction by pairing the functional clarity of classic wayfinding typography with an authentic Arabic voice. Grounded in balanced, open Naskh proportions, the typeface ensures swift comprehension for pedestrians navigating fast-moving concourses or drivers reading overhead directional boards.

Its low contrast architecture spans ten distinct styles, offering weights from Light and Regular through to Bold and Black & Ultra across both normal and condensed widths. This width range proves crucial when fitting dual-script instructions onto spatial hardware with fixed architectural footprints. Typographers can set Arabic text alongside English, German, or French without suffering uneven visual texture or clumsy optical mismatches. Because the glyph coverage also accommodates Persian and Urdu, regional transportation networks can preserve a unified aesthetic throughout multilingual terminals.

The same structural discipline translates naturally from physical enameled signs to digital terminal displays, companion mobile apps, and municipal branding systems. Its friendly, corporate tone keeps complex schedules and spatial diagrams orderly under harsh terminal lighting or cramped pixel densities. Distributed in OTF, TTF, WOFF, and WOFF2 formats, Frutiger Light Arabic provides designers with a consistent typographic tool built specifically for high-stress, high-clarity bilingual navigation.
